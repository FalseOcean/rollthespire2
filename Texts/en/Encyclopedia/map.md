# Maps: How Do You Describe a Good Map?

“This is a good map” is a natural judgment, but a difficult filter to define. A player who hunts Elites, one who wants safe development at Rest Sites, and one who seeks fewer fights and more random opportunities through question marks may judge the same map very differently.

That is why I have not made map filtering a single score. One number would struggle to serve these preferences or explain what makes a result desirable. Instead, I want to separate the map's properties so you can express the routes you want and the limits you will accept.

You can currently set properties for individual Acts, or a route objective for an Act or the whole run. For example, you might request a route with many Elites, or limit how many Rest Sites any route can visit. “Some route” and “every route” mean different things; [[map-routes]] explains minimum counts, maximum counts, and comparison directions. Other properties answer different questions, such as the unavoidable opening stretch of normal combats.

These conditions describe standard maps under your current configuration, without applying map-changing effects you might acquire later. Each Act's standard map uses an independent random state derived from the seed; route choices in an earlier Act do not advance the next Act's map generation. Inspect Act content such as {{act:OVERGROWTH}} and {{act:UNDERDOCKS}}, Ascension, and player count using the settings you intend to play.

The map workspace in Seed Information lets you browse, draw, and confirm routes. Once routes for the current and earlier Acts are confirmed, you can inspect ordinary question-mark room types under the premises explained on the page. A question-mark node is not a guaranteed event; whether a particular event can be encountered still depends on its queue and eligibility. See [[event-appearance]]. The in-run map tool instead starts from the live state in the current Act; see [[path-prediction|In-run path prediction]].

This is my current way of organizing map preferences. Suggestions for properties, route conditions, or layouts that better express what you want to play are welcome.
