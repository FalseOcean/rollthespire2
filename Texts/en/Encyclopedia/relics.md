# Relic Sequences: What Does a Position Tell You?

Like event queues, relic sequences provide relatively economical information to filter: for example, requiring a target first in a rarity's initial sequence, or among its first few positions. Determining what “the first Elite” will actually give also requires the reward's rarity, the state on arrival, and earlier consumption. This sequence alone cannot answer that question.

“First” means an **initial sequence position**, not the first relic actually obtained in the run. Common, Uncommon, and Rare are displayed from the front. When a reward requests that rarity, and whether other sources have consumed or removed relics, affect what you actually encounter.

Shop-exclusive relics also form a sequence. A normal shop's fixed Shop-exclusive slot is relatively stable, so I placed filtering by shop visit order in [[shop]]. This sequence is displayed in back-draw order, skipping relics that cannot be sold. It does not describe the shop's other two relic slots, which roll a rarity. See [[relic-shop-consumption]] for how shops consume Common, Uncommon, and Rare bags.

Treasure rooms use a separate bag: shared in multiplayer, and separate from the personal Common, Uncommon, and Rare bags in single-player too. There is now a target filter for the first two chests, alongside a nine-entry initial reference in Seed Information; these have different scopes and premises. The first relic in your personal Rare sequence cannot tell you what the first Rare chest will give; see [[relic-chests]].
